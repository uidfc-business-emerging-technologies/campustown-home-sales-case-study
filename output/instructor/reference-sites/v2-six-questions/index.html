Question 5. Assessment

How close is the assessed value to the sale price?

In of 11 areas the median assessed value is to of the sale price, but are far below. Both fit how those states assess property. Pennsylvania counties assess from irregularly updated "base year" values, and the state's own common level ratio factors put Centre County, home to Penn State, at about one sixth of market value. In Michigan, assessed value is set at half of market value, and taxable value can rise by no more than inflation or 5% a year until the home sells (Michigan State Tax Commission), so a ratio below 0.5 fits a capped value. sits at because California sets a new assessed value at the purchase price when a home changes hands (California State Board of Equalization), and every row here is a sale. Data notes

Every figure comes from the College-town home sales dataset (version 1.0): completed home sales around 11 university campuses, sold between and . The script scripts/build_data.py computes them, and the site reads its output, data/answers.json.

Campus area
The university column: the nearest of 11 campuses, assigned from each home's coordinates.
Valid sale
A sale price of $1,000 or more. This leaves out sales, including at $0, so sales are valid.
Price per square foot
Sale price divided by living area, computed for each valid sale with at least 100 square feet of living area ( sales), then the median of those values. We never divide a median price by a median area.
Days on market
Days from list_date to contract_date, for the sales with both dates. A missing date is never treated as zero, and we don't use sale_date, which would add about a month of closing time.
Distance bands
From miles_to_campus, the straight-line distance to a campus landmark: under 1 mile, 1 to under 2, 2 to under 5, 5 to under 10, and 10 or more.
Falls with distance (Question 3)
"At every step" means each band with enough sales is cheaper than the one before. "Overall" means the farthest band is cheaper than the nearest and no step rises by more than .
Listing window (Question 4)
Only homes listed in the window the case sets, , with all campus areas pooled together.
Far from 1 (Question 5)
A median ratio below 0.5, meaning the typical home is assessed at less than half its sale price. Every other area is at or above.
Small groups
A group with fewer than 20 sales shows "fewer than 20 sales" instead of a figure.
Medians
Every figure is a median, not a mean. Some prices in the data are implausible (up to ), and means would be pulled far off by them.
Assessment ratio
Assessed value divided by sale price, for the valid sales with an assessed value above 0.
Repeated addresses
Sales grouped by street address, city, state and ZIP code. We never remove a sale because its address repeats: in State College these are separate condo units, each with its own parcel number. Question 6 explains why many of the repeats around Virginia look like one sale recorded twice.

We don't use the home_type column, because the data description sheet shows it is unreliable.

Data: College-town home sales dataset, version 1.0 (2026). University of Illinois-Deloitte Foundation Center for Business Emerging Technologies. Data collected March 2026.
