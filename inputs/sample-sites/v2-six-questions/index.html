Question 1 · Price levels

For each campus area, what are the median sale price and the median price per square foot?

UC Davis is the most expensive campus area on both measures, and Illinois is the least expensive on both. The ranking barely changes between the two: only Florida and Georgia swap places. Georgia's median sale price is higher than Florida's, but its homes are larger (a median of 1,902 square feet, against 1,756), so it ranks lower on price per square foot.

Question 2 · Speed

Where do homes sell fastest, and how much is missing?

Homes near UW-Madison, Illinois, Michigan and Virginia go under contract fastest, at a median of 7 days. Georgia (27 days), Florida (25) and Iowa (23.5) are slowest. Days on market can't be computed for 16% to 35% of sales, depending on the area, because a list or contract date is missing. Those sales are disproportionately new construction and off-market sales, so each figure describes only the homes that were listed. Iowa (35% missing) and Penn State (33%) deserve the most caution.

Question 3 · Distance

Does price per square foot fall as the distance from campus grows?

Mostly yes: in 10 of 11 areas, homes within a mile of campus cost more per square foot than homes in the farthest distance band with enough sales. The fall is steady in five areas (Michigan, UNC, Virginia, Penn State and UC Davis), where every band is cheaper than the one before. Elsewhere it is uneven: in UW-Madison, Florida, Purdue, Georgia and Iowa, at least one farther band costs a little more than a nearer one. Illinois is the exception. It is cheapest per square foot 1 to 2 miles from campus ($137) and most expensive 5 to 10 miles out ($198).

Question 4 · Timing

Which listing months sell fastest, and which are slowest?

Homes listed in spring sell fastest: listings from March, April and May went under contract in a median of 7 days. The pace slows through summer and fall. Listings from December were slowest, at a median of 25 days, followed by November (20 days). Months combine every year in the data; nearly all of these listings are from 2023 to 2025.

Question 5 · Assessment

How close is the assessed value to the sale price?

In 9 of 11 areas the median assessed value is 78% to 100% of the sale price, but Penn State (0.18) and Michigan (0.33) are far below. Both fit how those states assess property. Pennsylvania counties assess from irregularly updated "base year" values, which can sit far below current prices. In Michigan, the assessed value is meant to be about half of market value, and taxable value can rise by no more than inflation or 5% a year until the home sells (Lake County, Michigan guide). UC Davis sits at 1.00 because California sets a new assessed value at current market value when a home changes hands (California Board of Equalization), and every row here is a sale. The other areas fall a little below 1, which is what we would expect if assessments trail recent sale prices.

Question 6 · Buildings

How many addresses have more than one sale, and how far are they from campus?

103 addresses had more than one sale, covering 240 sales, and two areas hold most of them: Virginia (80 addresses, 160 sales) and Penn State (13 addresses, 60 sales). Overall, these sales sit a little farther from campus than other sales (a median of 4.1 miles, against 3.4), but that is Virginia's pattern showing through. Its multi-sale addresses are a median 6.7 miles from campus, against 3.3 for its other sales. Penn State is the reverse: its multi-sale addresses are a median 0.5 miles from campus, against 2.2, which fits condo and apartment buildings close to campus. Purdue, Illinois and Iowa have none.

Data notes

Every figure comes from the College-town home sales dataset (version 1.0): 35,529 completed home sales around 11 university campuses, sold between March 27, 2023 and March 25, 2026. The script scripts/build_data.py computes them, and the site reads its output, data/answers.json.

Campus area
The university column: the nearest of 11 campuses, assigned from each home's coordinates.
Valid sale
A sale price of $1,000 or more. This leaves out 4 sales, including one at $0. 35,525 sales are valid.
Price per square foot
Sale price divided by living area, computed for each valid sale with at least 100 square feet of living area (35,021 sales), then the median of those values. We never divide a median price by a median area.
Days on market
Days from list_date to contract_date, for the 28,043 sales with both dates. A missing date is never treated as zero, and we don't use sale_date, which would add about a month of closing time.
Distance bands
From miles_to_campus, the straight-line distance to a campus landmark: under 1 mile, 1 to under 2, 2 to under 5, 5 to under 10, and 10 or more.
Small groups
A group with fewer than 20 sales shows "fewer than 20 sales" instead of a figure.
Medians
Every figure is a median, not a mean. Some prices in the data are implausible (up to $81.5 million), and means would be pulled far off by them.
Assessment ratio
Assessed value divided by sale price, for the 34,497 valid sales with an assessed value above 0.
Repeated addresses
Sales grouped by street address, city, state and ZIP code. These are separate condo or apartment units with no unit number in the data, so we never remove them as duplicates.

We don't use the home_type column, because the data description sheet shows it is unreliable.

Data: College-town home sales dataset, version 1.0 (2026). University of Illinois-Deloitte Foundation Center for Business Emerging Technologies. Data collected March 2026.
